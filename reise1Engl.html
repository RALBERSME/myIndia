The Secret of Jaigarh: India's Most Mysterious Treasure Hunt

High above the palaces of Jaipur stands the mighty Jaigarh Fort. It was deemed unconquerable and housed not only the world's largest cannon on wheels but also a dark secret: it was the treasury of the Maharajas of Jaipur.

For centuries, people whispered legends about the Kachwaha treasure. It was said that the rulers had hidden tons of gold bullion, diamonds, and emeralds deep within underground water tanks — plunder from successful military campaigns for the Mughal Empire in the 16th century. No one knew the exact location except the reigning Maharaja and a hand-picked guard of sentries.

he Day the Army Arrived

In 1976 — while India was in a politically extremely tense State of Emergency — the then Prime Minister of India, Indira Gandhi, decided to put an end to the myth. She suspected that the royal family of Jaipur was hiding unimaginable riches from the state.

On June 10, 1976 , the unthinkable happened: a special unit of the Indian Army completely sealed off Jaigarh Fort. Tourism was halted, and military experts moved in with metal detectors. For five months, soldiers dug up the fortress and drained the gigantic underground water reservoirs.

The Mysterious End and the Blocked Highway

After months of intensive excavations, the government abruptly called off the search in November 1976. The official statement read: absolutely nothing was found.

Yet, the citizens of Jaipur do not believe the story to this day. Right around the days the army withdrew, the Delhi-Jaipur Highway was completely closedto all public traffic — under the strictest military guard. Eyewitnesses reported a convoy of heavy military trucks driving out of the fortress deep in the night, escorted directly toward the Reserve Bank of India.

Was the legendary treasure secretly hauled away to prevent a national bankruptcy? Or was the search truly in vain? Jaigarh Fort remains steadfastly silent about its secret to this day.

Historical sources: Documented correspondence between I. Gandhi and Z. A. Bhutto (August 1976); Memoirs of Maharani Gayatri Devi; contemporary historical documentation from India Today & The Times of India.
