The Indian Caste System

A millennia-old social order between tradition, religion, and modernity.

What is the caste system & where does it come from?

he Indian caste system is one of the oldest social hierarchies in the world. It divides society into strictly segregated groups (castes). Historically, the system is based on two concepts:

  • Varna: The four classical main classes, which are already mentioned in the sacred Hindu scriptures (the Vedas around 1500 BC). The system is often explained symbolically through the body parts of the cosmic being, Purusha.
  • Jati: The actual sub-groups relevant to everyday life. There are thousands of Jatis, which are usually linked to specific professions, regions, and family groups.

How are you assigned to a caste?

Caste membership is determined exclusively by birth. You are born into a caste and remain there for life. Ascending or switching to a different caste through merit, marriage, or wealth is absolutely impossible in the traditional system. This is known as social immobility.

Rights, Duties, and Taboos

The traditional caste system regulated almost all aspects of daily coexistence through strict codes of purity and impurity:

What were the upper castes permitted to do?

  • Study religious texts and lead rituals (Brahmins).
  • Exercise political power and own land.
  • Claim access to all public wells, temples, and residential areas.

What prohibitions existed (especially for lower groups)?

  • Endogamy: It was strictly mandatory to marry only within one's own caste.
  • Commensality (Communal Eating): Higher castes were forbidden from accepting food from lower castes, as this was considered ritually "impure."
  • Untouchability: Dalits were often physically forbidden from even coming near higher castes; in some places, even their shadow was considered polluting.

Has the caste system been overcome by now?

The answer is complex: Legally yes, but in everyday social life, partially no.

The Legal Situation (Since 1950)

With the Indian Constitution of 1950, discrimination based on caste was officially banned by law. "Untouchability" is a punishable offense. Furthermore, India introduced a globally unique system of affirmative action (reservations), which guarantees government jobs, parliamentary seats, and university admissions for historically disadvantaged groups.

Modern Urban Society

In booming megacities like Mumbai, Delhi, or Bangalore, the caste system is rapidly losing its significance. In professional life, on the subway, or in restaurants, hardly anyone asks about caste. Education and economic success often carry more weight here than ancestral background.

Rural Areas & Marriage

In rural regions, however, where the majority of the Indian population lives, Jati structures still dictate everyday life and the social hierarchy. The most persistent relic is the marriage market: even today, over 90% of marriages in India are arranged within the same caste (often organized via specialized newspaper ads or apps).
