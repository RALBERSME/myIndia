Historical Development of the Religious Landscape

India is considered one of the most spiritual centers in the world and is the birthplace of four major religions: Hinduism, Buddhism, Jainismus, and Sikhism (known as the Dharmic religions). Over centuries, through trade, migration, and conquests, the Abrahamic religions (Judaism, Christianity, Islam) were also introduced.

  • From approx. 1500 BC: Development of the Vedic religion, the foundation of modern Hinduism.
  • 6th Century BC: Emergence of Buddhism and Jainism as reform movements against the rigid ritual system and the caste system of that era.
  • 1st Century AD: Christianity traditionally reaches India through Saint Thomas the Apostle on the Malabar Coast (Kerala).
  • From the 7th/8th Century AD: First contacts with Islam through Arab traders, followed by Muslim ruling dynasties (such as the Mughal Empire) starting in the 12th century.
  • 15th Century AD: Emergence of Sikhism in Punjab by Guru Nanak, as a synthesis and independent path distinct from Hinduism and Islam.

Religious Relations and Conflicts

The relationship among religions in India is marked by deep ambivalence. On one hand, a millennia-old principle of religious tolerance and syncretism exists—in daily life, people of different faiths often share festivals and holy sites. On the other hand, India's past and present are interspersed with severe religious tensions (communal conflicts or communal violence). The most painful turning point was the Partition of India in 1947 into secular India and Muslim Pakistan, which claimed millions of casualties and refugees.

Today, conflicts most frequently ignite between the Hindu majority and religious minorities:

  • Hindu-Muslim Conflicts: Politically and historically charged tensions that manifested in events such as the destruction of the Babri Masjid in Ayodhya (1992) or the Gujarat riots (2002).
  • Political Nationalism: The rise of Hindutva nationalism raises questions regarding the protection of India's secular constitution and the rights of Muslims and Christians.
  • The Caste System: Although legally banned, the social hierarchy, which was historically intertwined with religious practice, continues to shape social friction to this day, particularly for the Dalits (Untouchables).

1. Hinduism (Sanatana Dharma)

Approx. 79.8% of the population (approx. 1.1 billion people)

Distribution: Most prominent and widespread across almost all of India. The only exceptions with lower percentages are the far north (Punjab, Jammu & Kashmir) and parts of the northeast.

Where do humans come from? In their innermost core (Atman, the soul), they are a spark of the universal, divine consciousness (Brahman). The physical body is merely a temporary shell.
What is their purpose in life? Pursuing four life goals: Dharma (duty, ethics), Artha (prosperity), Kama (pleasure), and ultimately Moksha (liberation).
Where do humans go, death & rebirth? Death affects only the body. The soul is subject to reincarnation (Samsara), governed by the law of cause and effect (Karma). The ultimate goal is to escape this cycle (Moksha).
Core themes of life: Cosmic order (Dharma), spiritual practice (Yoga and Meditation), and devotion to the Divine (Bhakti).
Love, compassion & joy of life: Love is the highest spiritual power of connection. Compassion is rooted in the understanding that the same divine self resides in others as in oneself (Ahimsa: non-violence). Joy of life (Kama) is affirmed as a legitimate part of human existence, as long as it aligns with ethics.
A fulfilled life: When one has fulfilled one's duties toward family and society and attains inner peace through the realization of oneness with the Divine.

2. Islam

Approx. 14.2% of the population (approx. 200 million people)

Distribution: India houses one of the largest Muslim populations globally. It is particularly well-represented in Jammu & Kashmir, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, as well as in major cities like Hyderabad and Mumbai.

Where do humans come from? They are a creation of God (Allah), formed from clay, and were placed on earth as a trustee (Khalifa).
What is their purpose in life? Submission to the will of God, the worship of Allah, and leading a righteous life according to the Five Pillars of Islam.
Where do humans go, death & rebirth? There is no rebirth. Earthly life occurs only once. After physical death, the soul waits in the grave for the Day of Judgment. This is followed by eternal stay in Paradise (Jannah) or in Hell (Jahannam).
Core themes of life: Monotheism (Tawhid), justice, community (Ummah), and adherence to the divine law. In India, Sufism (mystical Islam) is also historically deeply rooted.
Love, compassion & joy of life: Love signifies the love for God and His creation. Compassion manifests practically in the mandatory almsgiving for the poor (Zakat). Joy of life is seen in the blessings of God (family, community, nature), embedded within a life pleasing to God.
A fulfilled life: A life in harmony with the commandments of God, which can stand before Allah's mercy on the Day of Judgment.

3. Christianity

Approx. 2.3% of the population (approx. 32 million people)

Distribution: Most prominent in the south (especially in Kerala and Goa due to deeply rooted, centuries-old traditions) as well as in the northeastern states such as Nagaland, Mizoram, and Meghalaya, where Christians constitute the majority of the population.

Where do humans come from? They are beings willed by God, created in His image. Every human being possesses an immortal soul and an inherent dignity, bestowed upon them directly through creation.
What is their purpose in life? Following Jesus Christ by fulfilling the Greatest Commandment of love: loving God with all one's heart and one's neighbor as oneself. This includes striving for justice, peace, and truth.
Where do humans go? Is there a death? Physical death is the end of earthly life, but not the end of existence. It is viewed as a transition into eternity. The soul enters into the judgment of God and hopes for the resurrection of the dead.
Is there a rebirth? No. In Christianity, earthly life is understood to be unique and unrepeatable. Reincarnation or a recurring cycle on earth is strictly rejected theologically.
What are the core themes of life? Faith (Fides), hope (Spes), and love (Caritas). Central concepts also include God's grace, the forgiveness of sins, reconciliation, and salvation through the cross and resurrection of Jesus.
What is love? The highest Christian virtue, defined as unconditional, sacrificial devotion (Agape). It is not a mere feeling, but a conscious act of will that even includes the love of enemies.
What is compassion? Active suffering with and helping others, inspired by the example of Jesus. It manifests in practical service to one's neighbor (Diakonia), especially for the poor, the sick, the marginalized, and the weak of society.
What is the joy of life? A gift from God and a fruit of the Holy Spirit. It springs from gratitude for life and deep trust in divine care (Providence), even in times of suffering.
What is a fulfilled life? A life led in close fellowship with God and in service to one's fellow human beings. It is measured not by material wealth, but by the depth of love and inner peace, sustained by the hope of eternal life.

4. Sikhism

Approx. 1.7% of the population (approx. 24 million people)

Distribution: Extremely heavily concentrated in the northwestern state of Punjab, which represents the historical, cultural, and spiritual home of the Sikhs. The central sanctuary, the Golden Temple in Amritsar, is also located here.

Where do humans come from? They originate directly from the source of the one, formless, timeless, and unborn creator God (Ik Onkar). The human soul carries the divine light deep within itself as an eternal spark.
What is their purpose in life? Leading an honest life through one's own, righteous labor (Kirat Karni), continuously remembering and meditating on the name of God (Naam Japna)), and sharing one's prosperity with the needy(Vand Chako). Asceticism and escapism are strictly rejected; humans are meant to live in society as active family individuals.
Where do humans go? Is there a death? Death only affects the transient biological shell. The immortal soul leaves the body and continues its journey. The ultimate goal after physical demise is the complete extinguishing of the ego and merging into the primal source of the Creator.
Is there a rebirth? Yes. Sikhism shares the concept of the cycle of rebirths (Samsara). An unpurified soul wanders through various life forms until it seizes the opportunity for spiritual liberation during human life, thereby ending the cycle.
What are the core themes of life? The absolute social equality of all human beings (complete abolition of caste barriers, gender hierarchies, or ancestry), overcoming the five inner thieves (lust, anger, greed, attachment, and pride), and combating one's own ego (Haumai).
What is love? The purest, most intense, and unadulterated form of longing for the Creator. In Sikhism, true love for God is revealed directly through practical, unconditional, and loving service to His entire creation.
What is compassion? A fundamental divine virtue(Daya) that must become visible in action. The most famous example is the institution of the Langar: in every Sikh temple, a free meal is served daily to all people, completely regardless of their religion, caste, or social class.
What is the joy of life? A consistent state of unwavering, positive fundamental optimism and trust in God's will (Chardi Kala) – even during the most severe crises, hardships, or periods of persecution.
What is a fulfilled life? A life as a "Saint-Soldier"(Sant-Sipahi): an existence that is internally entirely pure, spiritually awake, and turned toward God, while externally standing up courageously, uprightly, and actively for social justice and the protection of the weak.

5. Buddhism and Jainism

Buddhism (approx. 0.7%) | Jainism (approx. 0.4%)

Distribution: Today, Buddhism is primarily anchored in Maharashtra (due to the historical Dalit conversion movement led by Dr. B.R. Ambedkar), as well as in the Himalayan regions (Ladakh, Sikkim) and in exile communities such as Dharamshala. Jainism is heavily concentrated in the urban and economic centers in the west of the country, particularly in Gujarat, Rajasthan, and Mumbai.

Where do humans come from? There is no personified creator God in either system. The universe and human existence originate from a beginningless, cosmic cycle of becoming and passing away, which constantly sustains itself through unconscious causes, ignorance, and mental craving.
What is their purpose in life? In Buddhism: Overcoming existential suffering by perceiving reality exactly as it truly is, using the Noble Eightfold Path. In Jainism: The radical purification of the soul from all material attachments through extreme asceticism, truthfulness, and absolute, all-encompassing non-violence.
Where do humans go? Is there a death? Death is the mere disintegration of temporary physical and mental factors of existence. At that very moment, the remaining, unextinguished karmic potential shapes and conditions a new existence.
Is there a rebirth? Yes, both religions are fundamentally based on the law of karma and reincarnation. The goal in Buddhism is to attain Nirvana (the extinguishing of the flame of craving and suffering). The goal in Jainism is Moksha (the final liberation of the soul, which thereafter dwells in eternal bliss and infinite knowledge).
What are the core themes of life? Mental mindfulness, meditative discipline, the law of cause and effect (Karma), and absolute, uncompromising non-violence toward absolutely all living and sentient beings (Ahimsa) – a practice that goes so far in Jainism that even microorganisms and insects are meticulously protected in daily life.
What is love? In Buddhism, love is primarily understood as universal loving-kindness (Metta). It is the pure, selfless wish that all sentient beings may be happy, completely free from possessive attachment, jealousy, or personal expectations.
What is compassion? Active, deep compassion (Karuna) and the unceasing effort to alleviate the suffering of other beings. In Jainism, this compassion manifests in a rigorously vegan or vegetarian lifestyle to avoid harming a soul in any living creature.
What is the joy of life? At its core, it does not arise from material greed, sensory intoxication, or worldly consumerism, but from the deep inner freedom of the mind, emotional serenity (equanimity), and unshakable peace in the present moment.
What is a fulfilled life? Attaining complete spiritual awakening (enlightenment/Buddhahood) or leading an absolutely ethically flawless life that has never caused pain or harm to another living being at any moment of existence.
