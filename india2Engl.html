The Birth of a Subcontinent

From Prehistoric Drift to Modern Superpower

The formation of India is a geological and historical epic that began around 140 million years ago. Back then, the Indian tectonic plate broke away from the ancient supercontinent of Gondwana, embarking on one of the fastest tectonic journeys in Earth's history. Drifting northward at a speed of about 20 centimeters per year, the plate eventually collided with the Eurasian plate with brute force some 50 million years ago. This monumental collision was so immense that it buckled the Earth's crust, creating the Himalayas and the Tibetan Plateau — a natural fortress that continues to shape the climate and isolation of the Indian subcontinent to this day.

Sheltered by these mountains, human history awakened: over 4,500 years ago, one of humanity’s first advanced civilizations flourished in the fertile Indus Valley. Following millennia marked by the rise of powerful dynasties and the defining era of the Mughal Empire, the land fell under British colonial rule. Modern India, as we know it today, was ultimately born into independence on August 15, 1947 , following the tireless, non-violent resistance led by Mahatma Gandhi. It is the fascinating fusion of this primeval force of nature and thousands of years of cultural history that makes India one of the most dynamic and multifaceted countries on our planet today.
