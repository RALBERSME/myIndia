India's Neighbors

A historical and geopolitical overview of relations, conflicts, and cultural ties across the South Asian subcontinent.

Pakistan

Historically Strained
Cultural Similarities & Differences

Historically and ethnically, North Indians and Pakistanis share the same DNA, language (Hindi and Urdu are linguistically almost identical), cuisine, clothing, and music. The primary difference is religion: Pakistan was deliberately created in 1947 as a homeland for the Muslims of the subcontinent, while India remained secular (with a Hindu majority).

Past Relations & Causes of Conflict

Prior to 1947, these peoples lived together within the British colonial empire. The hasty and violent Partition of India in 1947 ed to massive religious riots, creating millions of refugees and resulting in up to one million deaths. The main bone of contention was and remains the region of Kaschmir, which is claimed by both countries.

Wars & Disputes

There have been four major military conflicts: the First Kashmir War (1947), , the Second Kashmir War (1965),, the Bangladesh War (1971), in which India supported the secession of East Pakistan, and the Kargil War (1999). Today, both states possess nuclear weapons, which keeps the conflict permanently frozen but highly dangerous.

Present-Day Relations

Relations are largely deadlocked. Official trade and diplomacy are almost completely frozen. Border incidents and proxy conflicts (allegations of terrorism) continuously strain the relationship.

China (Tibet)

Geopolitical Rivalry
Cultural Similarities & Differences

These are two completely different civilizations. India is Indo-Aryan/Dravidian and spiritually heavily shaped by Hinduism; China is East Asian and shaped by Confucianism/Buddhism. The historical bridge between the two countries was the peaceful spread ofBuddhism from India to China via the Silk Road.

Past Relations & Causes of Conflict

For millennia, the Himalayas separated the two empires, meaning there were virtually no direct military conflicts. This changed radically when China annexed Tibet in 1950. India granted asylum to the Dalai Lama 1959. The primary cause of dispute is the imprecise, colonial border in the high mountains (the McMahon Line).

Wars & Disputes

In the Sino-Indian Border War of 1962, India suffered a severe defeat; China occupied the Aksai Chin region. To this day, lethal skirmishes between soldiers using clubs and stones (to avoid escalation) repeatedly occur in the regions of Ladakh and Arunachal Pradesh.

Present-Day Relations

A mixture of intense economic interdependence (billions in trade) and sharp geopolitical rivalry. China is trying to strategically encircle India (including through ports in Pakistan and Sri Lanka), while India is expanding its alliances with the West.

Bangladesch

Close but Complex
Cultural Similarities & Differences

Extremely high similarity. The people of Bangladesh share a language, literature (such as the works of Rabindranath Tagore), culture, and history with the inhabitants of the Indian state of West Bengal. The difference here, too, lies primarily in religion (Bangladesh is majority Muslim, West Bengal is Hindu).

Past Relations & Wars

The country was part of Pakistan until 1971 as "East Pakistan." Because West Pakistan brutally oppressed the Bengalis, India intervened militarily in the 1971 War and helped Bangladesh achieve its independence. India was initially celebrated as a liberator.

Points of Contention & Present-Day Relations

Points of contention include illegal migration into India, the construction of border fences by India, and the fair sharing of water resources from the Ganges River. Following years of stable economic cooperation, domestic political upheavals in Bangladesh are currently causing a more cautious and wait-and-see attitude on both sides.

Nepal & Bhutan

Traditionally Friendly
Cultural Similarities & Differences

Nepal is historically and culturally deeply intertwined with India (both are majority Hindu and share similar languages). Bhutan is Buddhist-dominated and has Tibetan roots, but shares a long tradition of spiritual exchange with India.

Past and Present-Day Relations

India has always maintained open borders with Nepal; millions of Nepalis work in India visa-free. Bhutan is considered India's closest ally in the region—historically, India even handled aspects of Bhutan's foreign and defense policies. There have never been wars between these states.

Current Challenges

Both Nepal and Bhutan are increasingly catching China's attention. China is trying to expand its influence through infrastructure projects, which is viewed with great concern in India and leads to diplomatic tensions.

Sri Lanka

Neighboring Ties
Cultural Similarities & Wars

The majority of Sri Lanka's population (Sinhalese) and the minority (Tamils) originally trace their descent back to the Indian subcontinent. India intervened in the civil war there (1987–1990) with a "peacekeeping force" to disarm the separatist Tamils (LTTE). This ended in a fiasco and led to the 1991 assassination of former Indian Prime Minister Rajiv Gandhi by a female Tamil suicide bomber.

Present-Day Relations

Today, India and Sri Lanka cooperate closely. India helped Sri Lanka during its recent severe economic crisis with massive loans and relief supplies in order to curb China's growing maritime influence on the island.
