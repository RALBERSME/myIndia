Where does Vedic Mathematics come from?

Vedic Mathematics is a system of mental arithmetic that was reconstructed between 1911 and 1918 by Swami Bharati Krishna Tirtha (an Indian scholar and spiritual leader). He studied ancient Indian Sanskrit texts (the Vedas) and extracted an astonishingly efficient mathematical system from them.

The entire system is based on just 16 Sutras (core formulas) and 13 sub-Sutras. Rather than describing new mathematical laws, these formulas provide highly optimized ways for our brains to recognize and process patterns in numbers.

Why is it so extremely fast to use?

In conventional school mathematics, we calculate rigidly from right to left, write down numerous intermediate steps, and often use time-consuming standard algorithms. Vedic Mathematics breaks these rules:

(1) Patterns instead of rote memorization

It utilizes the brain's natural ability to recognize visual symmetries and patterns in sequences of numbers.

(2) Row-by-row calculation

Most calculations are solved in just a single row – often directly from left to right.

(3) Flexibility

Highly specialized shortcuts exist for various number combinations, drastically minimizing the computational effort required.

How does multiplying by 11 to 15 work?

o multiply any number by the numbers 11 through 15, Vedic Mathematics uses an ingenious principle: Mentally place a zero before and after the number and work your way through from right to left.

✖️ Multiplication by 11 ("Add the neighbor")

Rule: Take each digit and add its right-hand neighbor.

Example: 11 × 134
• Think of the number as: 0 1 3 4 0
• 4 + 0 = 4
• 3 + 4 = 7
• 1 + 3 = 4
• 0 + 1 = 1
• EResult read from right to left: 1474

✖️ Multiplication by 12 ("Double and add the neighbor")

Rule: Double each digit and add its right-hand neighbor.

Example: 12 × 24 (Think of 0 2 4 0)
• (4 × 2) + 0 = 8
• (2 × 2) + 4 = 8
• (0 × 2) + 2 = 2
• Result: 288

✖️ Multiplication by 13, 14, and 15 (The general principle)

The system continues exactly this way. You multiply the digit by the ones place of the multiplier and add the neighbor:

  • By 13: Triple the digit and add the right-hand neighbor.
  • By 14: Quadruple the digit and add the right-hand neighbor.
  • By 15: Quintuple the digit and add the right-hand neighbor.
  • By 16, 17, 18, 19: Multiply the digit by 6, 7, 8, or 9 respectively and add the right-hand neighbor.

⚠️ important for carries: If a step yields a result of 10 or more (e.g., 14), you write down the 4 and carry the 1 over to the next step on the left!
