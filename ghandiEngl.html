Mahatma Gandhi

"Be the change you wish
to see in this world."

Life & Origin

Born: 2. October 2, 1869, in Porbandar, Gujarat (British India).

Died: January 30, 1948, in New Delhi (at the age of 78, by assassination).

Born Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi into a wealthy merchant family, he later studied law in London. His life path led him to South Africa, where he first campaigned against racial segregation, before returning to India to fundamentally change the fate of his homeland.

What He Did & His Achievements

Satyagraha & Ahimsa

Gandhi developed the concept of Satyagraha (insistence on truth) and Ahimsa (absolute non-violence). He proved to the world that powerful empires can be overthrown without weapons.

The Salt March (1930)

A milestone of protest: Gandhi marched 385 kilometers to the sea to make his own salt. This broke the British salt monopoly and mobilized millions of Indians into civil disobedience.

The "Quit India" Movement (1942)

He demanded the full and immediate independence of India from the British Crown. Despite mass arrests, the momentum of the non-violent movement could no longer be stopped.

Commitment to the Untouchables

In addition to fighting the colonial rulers, Gandhi vehemently advocated for the lowest caste of the Indian caste system. He called them "Harijans" (Children of God) and fought for their human rights.

Significance for India & the World

Father of the Nation: In India, Gandhi is reverently referred to as Bapu (Father) or Mahatma (Great Soul). He is considered the architect of Indian independence, which was achieved on August 15, 1947.

His birthday, October 2, is now a national holiday in India (Gandhi Jayanti) and the International Day of Non-Violence worldwide. His principle of peaceful resistance inspired later freedom fighters such as Martin Luther King Jr. and Nelson Mandela.
