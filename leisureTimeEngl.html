Masti, Maza & Leisure Time!

Experience the passion, community, and joy with which India celebrates its free time.

In India, leisure time is rarely a solitary affair. It means „Maza“ (fun) and „Masti“ (lighthearted mischief) – preferably shared with a large circle of family, neighbors, and friends. Dive into the colorful facets!

The Most Popular Leisure Activities

Absolute Religion

Gully Cricket (Street Cricket)

In India, cricket is not just a sport, but a passionate, everyday religion. Whether in narrow city alleyways (gullies), on dusty village squares, or on the beach: as soon as there are a few free minutes, children and adults whip out improvised bats and play together.

Big Screen

The Bollywood Cult & Cinemas

Going to the cinema in India is an interactive leisure experience! You don't sit quietly in the dark: during epic scenes or songs featuring favorite stars, the audience claps, cheers loudly, sings along, and dances. At home, the entire family watches hours-long movies and celebrates the music.

Socializing

Chai Breaks & "Adda" (Gossiping)

athering casually at small roadside stalls (tapris) for a freshly brewed, sweet spiced tea (masala chai) is the ultimate social glue. Intensely debating politics, cricket, and life is affectionately called "Adda" – the art of cozy, leisurely time-wasting.

Classics

Carrom, Ludo & Cards

On hot afternoons or during the monsoon rains, leisure time moves indoors. The fast-paced finger billiards game Carrom alongside Ludo (the original form of Ludo/Parcheesi) and intense rounds of Rummy cards, unite grandparents and grandchildren around the table for hours.

High in the Sky

Patang Bazi (Kite Flying)

Particularly in western and northern India, rooftops transform into colorful festival zones. When flying kites, people engage in passionate duels in the sky, trying to cut neighbors' kites with their own glass-coated strings, accompanied by loud triumphant shouts ("Kai Po Che!").

Culinary Fun

Evening Market Strolls & Chaat

As soon as the heat of the day subsides, families flock to the bazaars. It is not always about shopping, but about seeing and being seen. The evening is crowned by sharing spicy, sweet, and sour snacks (chaat like pani puri or samosas) right by the roadside.
