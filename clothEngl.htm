Traditional Garments & Their Roots

The origins of Indian clothing stretch far back into history. Archaeological findings from the Indus Valley Civilization (c. 2500 BCE) prove that artfully draped fabrics were already in use back then. Since needles and the art of tailoring were virtually non-existent in the early eras, unstitched, wrapped clothing dominated, adapting perfectly to the hot climate.

The Saree (Sari)



Origin: The Saree is considered one of the oldest continuously worn garments in the world (mentioned in the Vedas around 1500 BC). It consists of a single piece of fabric measuring 5 to 9 meters in length.

How it is worn today: It is still worn today by millions of women of all ages—mostly made of cotton for everyday wear, and crafted from the finest silk with gold threads (Zari) for weddings.

Salwar Kameez & Lehengas



Origin: The Salwar Kameez (trousers and tunic) originates from Central Asia and brought to India by Muslim dynasties. The Lehenga (a richly ornamented, long skirt with a top) has its roots in the Mughal era.

How it is worn today: The Salwar Kameez is the most popular and comfortable everyday wear in Northern India. Lehengas serve as the classic festive and bridal attire.

Dhoti & Kurta



Origin: The Dhoti is an unstitched cloth wrapped around the legs and hips, deeply rooted in ancient India. It is frequently combined with a Kurta (a long shirt).

How it is worn today: In everyday life, it is primarily worn by older men and priests in rural regions, as well as in Southern India. For festivals and weddings, the Kurta is the standard nationwide.

Sherwani



Origin: A form-fitting, knee-length frock coat that developed in the 19th century as formal attire for the aristocracy in the Mughal Empire and under British influences.

How it is worn today: Exclusively festive and wedding attire. It is considered the most elegant garment for an Indian groom.

Cultivation & Natural Resources



Historically, India is thehome of cotton. The plant has been cultivated on the Indian subcontinent for millennia. Today, India is one of the largest cotton producers worldwide, with cultivation concentrated in the so-called "Cotton Belt" (states such as Gujarat and Maharashtra).

Besides cotton,silk plays an immense role. Regions like Varanasi (Benares) and Kanchipuram are world-famous for their traditional silk weaving mills. Jute is predominantly grown in the humid eastern region (West Bengal).

Manufacturing Process



Processing ranges from centuries-old manual labor to highly modern factories. Despite industrialization, millions of people work in the handloom sector (hand weaving). Traditional techniques such as block printing, Ikat weaving, and intricate embroideries (Chikan, Zardozi) are living crafts passed down from generation to generation.

The Transformation of the Textile Industry & Environmental Impacts

The Indian textile industry has transformed from a decentralized structure of village artisans into the country's second-largest employer (after agriculture) and a global export giant. However, this massive rise demands a heavy toll from nature.

Environmental Impact in the Past +

Historically speaking, textile production in India was ecologically sustainable. Clothing was woven from local natural fibers. Dyeing was done exclusively with plant-based and mineral substances (such as indigo, turmeric, or pomegranate peels). The wastewater was biodegradable, and the artisanal production volumes remained manageable.

Environmental Impact Today+

Today, the textile sector is one of the country's largest polluters. The excessive use of chemical pesticides in conventional cotton farming has heavily depleted soils and groundwater. Furthermore, unregulated dye houses in textile hubs (such as Tirupur or Surat) discharge unfiltered, toxic heavy metals and chemical dyes into rivers. The rise of synthetic fibers (polyester) further exacerbates the microplastic problem.

The Trend Toward Sustainability +

In response to the crisis, the market for organically grown cotton and "Khadi" (hand-spun, eco-friendly cotton, once promoted by Mahatma Gandhi as a symbol of resistance) is expanding. An increasing number of contemporary fashion labels are focusing on slow fashion and the recycling of secondhand clothing.

Significance of Clothing in Modern Society

In India, clothing is a direct indicator of social status, religion, regional origin, and modernity. In today's urban everyday life, an "Indo-Western" mix dominates: young people wear jeans paired with traditional Kurtas. Purely Western business attire (suits, pantsuits) is standard in the modern corporate world.

For festive occasions such as weddings, religious celebrations (like Diwali), or family gatherings, however, all generations without exception dress up in the most opulent traditional garments. It is regarded as a sign of respect and cultural pride.

Is Modeling Frowned Upon? & Famous Models

In the past, the modeling and fashion industry was viewed critically in moral terms by conservative families and was partially frowned upon. This has changed radically due to the global success of Indian beauties. Today, modeling is a highly respected, extremely competitive career path that frequently serves as a springboard into the Indian film industry (Bollywood).

Famous Contemporary Models:

  • Pooja Mor: Internationally successful on the runways of Paris and New York (working for Louis Vuitton, among others).

  • Pratik Shetty: One of India's most successful male models, booked for global campaigns.
  • Avanti Nagrath: Considered one of the top models of today and made history as the first Indian model to open runway shows for major European luxury brands (such as Versace).

Famous Indian Fashion Labels

India's haute couture blends traditional craftsmanship with cutting-edge silhouettes, achieving global success:

  • Sabyasachi (Sabyasachi Mukherjee): The undisputed king of Indian bridal wear. His monumental, traditional designs are ultimate status symbols and dress the biggest celebrities.
  • Manish Malhotra: Became famous as the head costume designer for the biggest Bollywood blockbusters. His fashion represents glamour, glitter, and contemporary luxury.
  • Anita Dongre: Renowned for sustainable high fashion that empowers traditional rural female artisans while making Indian aesthetics wearable for everyday life.
